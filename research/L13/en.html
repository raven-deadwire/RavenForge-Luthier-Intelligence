L13 — Technology as Mediation

How technology organizes a player’s perception, action, and judgment

2026-09-27 · v1.1 · Literature/citation verification revision / empirical validation pending

Document purposeInstrument geometry, electronics, controls, and DSP are not treated merely as channels for an already completed musical intention. Starting from Don Ihde and Peter-Paul Verbeek, this log asks what technologies make perceptible and which actions they facilitate or impede. It builds on bodily incorporation in L02 and action possibilities in L03, while making relations among perception, action, representation, timing, and recovery a distinct design concern. Multiscale geometry, active EQ/filter systems, and Chimera ground a proposed specification and evaluation framework.

Current design propositionGood mediation does not mean that technology has no effect. It means designing a relation that supports intended musical action while allowing its effects and constraints to be inspected, revised, and, where feasible, reversed. This is a RavenForge proposal, not a proven universal law.

1. Research question — designing the relation, not only transmission

A linear player → instrument → sound path is useful when reading a design. During performance, however, the audible result informs the next touch, dynamic change, mute, or setting adjustment. L13 therefore evaluates a relation involving a particular player, skill history, instrument state, task, and environment rather than an isolated component. The schematic below is an analytical recording aid, not a measured psychological model.

This connection preserves the scope of the existing L02 and L03 logs [R1–R2]. Verbeek describes mediation through the role of technology in shaping human–world relations [2]. L13 does not reduce this to another name for “EQ changes timbre.” Even for an identical signal transformation, control location, naming, reference settings, and the performance task raise separate questions about the judgments a design supports.

2. Ihde’s relations — analytical lenses, not product categories

Ihde’s embodiment, hermeneutic, alterity, and background relations provide starting distinctions for human–technology relations [1]. The instrument examples below are L13 applications. One device may participate in several relations; its actual relation cannot be established from appearance or a feature list alone.

RelationInstrument/technology interpretationUnwarranted conclusion
EmbodimentPracticed operation lets attention move toward phrasing.Familiarity or low weight makes it transparent for everyone.
HermeneuticA tuner, level meter, or band display is read to judge sound.The display presents the complete meaning of the sound.
AlterityNegotiation with an interactive system encountered as a quasi-other.Any menu or complicated interface is an alterity relation.
BackgroundPower, monitoring, or automatic processes condition activity without usual attention.What is unnoticed has no musical consequence.

3. Non-neutrality is not technological determinism

Ihde’s designer-fallacy argument cautions against deriving use and meaning solely from the designer’s intention [3]. Applied to instruments, this blocks the inference that adding a control necessarily makes a player perform in the intended way. Multistability concerns different stable relations of use across practices, not the number of presets.

Design rule 01Do not record intended and observed use in the same field. The former is a design hypothesis; the latter is an observation. Evaluate unexpected use for expressive possibility, confusion, risk, and recovery cost before labeling it an error.

4. Verbeek’s extension and the limits of RavenForge’s adoption

Verbeek criticizes treating mediation merely as an intermediate process between pre-given humans and a world. His stronger claim concerns their constitution through relations [4]. L13’s input/output diagrams are engineering decompositions for recordkeeping, not models that empirically prove or refute this philosophical position.

Influencing action must be distinguished from intending or being accountable in the human sense. L13 analyzes causal and relational roles without assigning independent moral responsibility to an instrument. This is RavenForge’s limited methodological commitment, not a summary verdict on Verbeek’s philosophy. His work on design ethics also makes the decision to influence conduct through technology an object of scrutiny [5].

5. Keeping three senses of transparency separate

L13 analytical distinctionQuestionRequired evidence
Experiential transparencyDoes operating the device demand intrusive attention during playing?Task observations and accounts of experience
Signal transparencyWhat changes relative to a specified reference signal path?Load, gain, response, nonlinearity, and delay measurements
Operational legibilityCan the path, state, and effects be understood and recovered when needed?State-identification and reference-restoration tasks

These three headings are L13’s practical distinction, not Ihde’s original taxonomy. A well-practiced distortion pedal may recede from operational attention while intentionally transforming the signal. Conversely, small signal changes can coexist with confusing hidden state transitions. These are hypotheses to examine, not efficacy claims for a particular product.

6. Case A — multiscale as a design relation, not only a length difference

Treating multiscale as mediation does not replace physical explanation with philosophical language. First record vibrating length by string, fret positions, neutral fret, and bridge/nut coordinates. Then examine the reaches and movements this geometry requires for a particular posture and technique. A defined fretboard geometry does not itself establish a bodily benefit for a player [R1–R2].

Calculated example — not measured dataRearranging the ideal flexible-string fundamental relation f=(1/2L)√(T/μ) gives T = 4μL²f². Under the explicit assumptions of identical linear density μ, identical fundamental frequency f, and ideal flexible-string behavior, 37 versus 34 inches gives (37/34)² ≈ 1.184. The approximately 18.4% figure is therefore an idealized length-only calculation. It does not include real core/winding construction, stiffness and other non-idealities, break angle, setup, or playing feel, and it does not establish an effect size for comfort or expression.

A later comparison must distinguish deliberately changed variables from those controlled or recorded. Log tuning, string specification, action, support position, posture, task, and familiarization. Where these cannot all be matched, disclose residual confounding. Do not call a comparison double-blind when the geometry remains visible or palpable. Narrow the outcome from “multiscale superiority” to access, errors, discomfort, and adaptation cost for the intended task.

7. Case B — active EQ/filter systems and control mapping

Hunt, Wanderley, and Paradis treat the mapping between performer controls and sound variables as a central concern in electronic instrument design [6]. Their paper concerns mapping in electronic-instrument interfaces; it is not a direct study of onboard analog EQ. L13 therefore extends the principle only as an analytical framework for documenting control-to-signal relations in onboard circuits. Specify not only knob count but the relation between travel and change, reference positions, interactions, input conditions, and state. This does not prove that a particular mapping has superior expressive value.

Record layerEQ/filter specificationVerification question
Signal pathPickup load, stage order, gain, filter response, output/power conditionsDoes the actual circuit state match the record?
Control mappingPot law, frequency/Q range, reference position, coupling/switchingWhat change follows a given movement?
User interpretationNames, markings, reference positions, state indicationDo expected effects match behavior?
Action outcomeReaching a target timbre, live adjustment, reference recoveryHow do errors, time, and expressive choices differ?

A06, B06, and A07 already separate input boundaries, stage order, reference/bypass states, and supply conditions [R3–R5]. A central EQ position should not be named “the passive original” before the comparison path is defined. Active-flat and passive bypass are not assumed identical. An 18 V label specifies supported supply/operating choices; it does not automatically establish a better playing relation.

Design rule 02Whether a signal change is intended, whether operation is legible, and whether it helps a musical task are three questions. One frequency-response plot cannot answer all three.

8. Case C — separating Chimera routing from its representation

BN01, dated September 23, 2026, records a development problem in which a shared screen template obscured differences among Classic, Dual, and Matrix. It describes one full-range route, two parallel full-range routes, and three split-band routes respectively, and records the development of band-relative Band Tone for Matrix [R6]. This is a historical design record, not evidence of the current build or measured user performance.

L13’s hypothesis is not that a more attractive UI is better. With the signal path held fixed, alternative representations can be compared for route-identification errors and reference-recovery time. Changing routing simultaneously confounds sonic and representational effects. Nor should band-limited input be assumed to remain band-limited after nonlinear processing. State whether a band label describes pre-processing input responsibility or final output bandwidth.

9. Mapping also requires state and history

Chadabe identifies limits of fixed mapping descriptions for interactive systems whose algorithms generate control information [7]. His argument does not directly validate preset restoration or stateful DSP in general. L13 extends the concern into a design hypothesis that mode transitions and state history should be documented; that extension is RavenForge’s interpretation. Record saving/restoration, hidden parameters, smoothing, and transition states. Whether values survive Matrix → Classic → Matrix and whether display and actual values agree cannot be answered by a description of one knob alone.

10. Timing, failure, and recovery belong in the relation

McPherson, Jack, and Moro’s 2016 action–sound latency study empirically compares digital-music-system configurations and shows that actual end-to-end latency depends on interfaces, buffers, software, and related configuration details rather than being exhausted by a nominal buffer setting [8]. Its numerical measurements belong to those specific hardware/software setups, not to current hardware or Chimera. L13 adopts only the methodological requirement to define endpoints and measure each configuration; it does not treat any millisecond figure in the paper as a universal perceptual threshold for every player and task.

A later bench evaluation should fix sample rate, buffers, algorithms, routing, CPU-load condition, and measurement boundaries. Report repeated-response medians, upper quantiles, maxima, dropouts, and missing responses together. Predefine exclusions and preserve raw records. Variation here means variation in repeated response time, not electrical sample-clock jitter.

12. Myth audit — what mediation must not conceal

Do not use “technology is either neutral or fully determining,” “expression grows with option count,” “familiar means signal-transparent,” “a valid physical model proves comfort,” or “one mean delay describes responsiveness” as design conclusions. Each omits relations, tasks, references, or distributions. Conversely, mediation is not permission to inflate every difference into significance. A small or task-irrelevant effect is also a possible result.

13. Proposed validation — separate bench behavior from playing

This is a proposal for later execution. No experiment, interview, or listening test was conducted for L13, and the decision to defer C-series work remains unchanged. Freeze the claim, task, and conditions before selecting tests. Instead of an overall “good mediation” score, prerecord the cost or possibility of a specific relation being evaluated.

StageExecution/recordInterpretive limit
A. Freeze specificationDrawing/circuit/build version, routing, input/load/power, reference, taskDo not claim reproducibility without change records.
B. BenchCheck paths/states, signal response, delay distribution, bypass/transitions/recoveryCircuit correctness is not usability evidence.
C. Playing taskWithin-participant comparisons, counterbalanced order, logged familiarization/environmentDisclose unresolved skill, order, and learning effects.
D. ExperienceRetrospective interview after timed tasks; predicted versus actual behaviorSeparate disruption caused by concurrent think-aloud.
E. ReportCompletion, errors, recovery, response distributions, and experience separatelySmall samples are exploratory; non-significance is not equivalence.

Playback comparisons can address perceived sonic differences but do not validate the real-time hand–instrument–sound relation. Record level matching as a listening-test condition without using compensation to conceal actual gain changes or clipping. Avoid giving only one system the favorable practical conditions. Set sample size and tolerances from the target effect and pilot findings; do not invent a universal participant count for “validation complete.”

15. Current design position and next research

L13’s output is not a declaration that technology replaces the human. It is a more specific way to record design responsibility. Specifications should explain behavior, representations should describe it honestly, and intended musical action should be evaluated alongside unexpected use. Present defaults as starting points rather than unique correct answers. Disclose chosen constraints and irreversible changes.
